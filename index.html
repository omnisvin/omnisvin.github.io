OmniSVIn achieves robust underwater SLAM with a single compact, lightweight, and affordable omnidirectional action camera.

Abstract

Underwater visual–inertial SLAM (VI-SLAM) is challenged by low visibility, turbidity, color distortion, dynamic illumination, and insufficient texture. We present OmniSVIn, to our knowledge the first fully omnidirectional underwater VI-SLAM system. It processes raw images from a dual-opposing-fisheye action camera using the double-sphere model, preserving 360 degree coverage without remapping or field-of-view loss. Deferred landmark promotion and finite-landmark retention improve landmark persistence and robustness in spite of uncertain underwater observations. Opposing-fisheye tracking and keyframe selection policy, together with cross-camera loop closure, exploit complementary views for steady tracking and full surround revisits to improve global map consistency.

We also introduce OmniAqua, the first underwater omnidirectional VI-SLAM dataset, of hours-long challenging cave and shipwreck visual–inertial sequences. On OmniAqua, OmniSVIn maintained uninterrupted tracking and produced globally consistent trajectory estimates and landmark maps. These results demonstrate that robust underwater SLAM is achievable with one compact, lightweight, affordable, omnidirectional action camera.

Approach

OmniSVIn System Overview. Our system augments SVIn2, a classical nonlinear optimization-based sonar-depth-visual-inertial SLAM framework, comprising a frontend and a backend. The components with blue background indicate our key contributions.

In the frontend, whole FoV is preserved thanks to the Double Sphere (DS) camera model since data admission. Due to the higher per pixel angular uncertainty of fisheye images as compared to pinhole images and temporary false correspondences in the underwater visual observations, low parallax observations remain pending while accumulating enough observations with sufficient parallax before triggering multi-view landmark promotion. At least three tracks are then required for a successful triangulation to ensure only well-constrained landmarks enter factor graph optimization.

In the backend, a landmark retention policy is proposed to maintain enough established landmarks for longer-term tracking despite visual degradation in underwater environments, without the need of extending the local window size impractically. Without the retention, when a keyframe is marginalized, the landmarks associated with it without new observations in the temporal window will also be marginalized and become unmatchable, whereas the retention mechanism keeps the landmarks and their factors explicit as long as surviving observations currently exist, and this provides sources for future observations to reacquire association.

Finally, cross-camera loop closure detection and DS-based geometric verification is performed asynchronously to achieve full-surrounding-aware revisit recognition and global map consistency.

OmniAqua SLAM Dataset

All the sequences are captured with a single Insta360 X5 dual-fisheye action camera in its official underwater Pro housing, which is compact, lightweight, and affordable. The camera is mounted on a handheld rig or diver propulsion vehicle (DPV) to capture the underwater scenes. The dataset includes hours-long challenging sequences from various underwater environments, e.g. caves and shipwrecks, featuring varying illumination, insufficient texture, low visibility, and dynamic scene elements.

Sequences from the OmniAqua SLAM dataset. Columns from left to right correspond to Stavronikita, Escondido, Tajma Ha and Otoch Ha.

We provide both the raw 5.7K 30 FPS dual-fisheye recordings (.insv and .lrv) from the Insta360 X5 camera and the well-synchronized visual-inertial ROS2 bags, along with calibration results. Our Insta360 telemetry extraction and synchronization tool is also open sourced to benefit the research community.

Results

We evaluate OmniSVIn on the OmniAqua SLAM dataset. Raw 5.7K video is downsampled to 832×832px at its original 30 FPS to reduce the computational load without losing visual details, while the synchronized IMU data retain its 1000 Hz sampling rate from the Insta360 X5 recordings. Static masks are also applied to exclude image regions occupied by the diver or DPV (diver propulsion vehicle) holding the camera.
